## 1. Critical infrastructure and sensitive sites (fires, explosions, vandalism, sabotage, photography/drone arrests, land purchases, surveillance devices)

### Takeaway
No 2026 incident in Canada that an official, expert or reputable outlet tied to foreign sabotage of infrastructure was found. The two most notable physical cases involving sensitive material or sites are domestic and unresolved as to motive: a weapons-and-explosives case among Western University students that drew in the RCMP's national security team, and the deadly training-area fire at CFB Wainwright, for which officials reportedly saw no sign of terrorism.

### Cited Findings
- London, Ont.: at about 1:40 a.m. on 24 January, Western University special constables arrested a trespasser after a foot chase; London police found a loaded handgun, then hazardous chemicals that could be used to make explosives at a residence near Chesham Avenue and Chesham Place; four people aged 27, 26, 25 and 21 were charged — [Global News, 28 Jan](https://globalnews.ca/news/11641729/london-police-firearm/)

### Inferences

- Two range fires at two bases within eleven days (Wainwright, Gagetown) point to a seasonal live-fire risk rather than to any hostile act.

## 2. Drones and aerial anomalies (bases, airports, prisons, nuclear plants, ports; unidentified objects; GPS/GNSS interference)

### Takeaway
No specific, publicly reported unexplained drone incident over a Canadian base, airport, prison, nuclear plant or port in 2026 was found. What is documented is the official response (counter-drone systems at naval and air bases, a DND purchase notice citing "unauthorized drone activity") and very large numbers of unauthorized hobby-type flights around World Cup venues, which police treated as a safety problem, not a security threat.

### Cited Findings
- CBC reported on 7 May, as summarized by DroneXL, that the navy now runs counter-drone systems at its bases, that the RCAF is protecting airfields due to host F-35 and P-8 aircraft, and that Vice-Admiral Angus Topshee said Canada has "some capability" but that telling a hostile drone from a civilian one over Halifax Harbour is hard; the Aeronautics Act was amended earlier in 2026 to strengthen authority to interdict unauthorized drones; no specific Canadian incident is described — [DroneXL, 7 May](https://dronexl.co/2026/05/07/canada-counter-drone-defenses-naval-bases-halifax/)
- DND posted a notice on 28 September seeking a system to protect personnel, equipment and facilities across Canada from drone attacks; details are restricted to qualified suppliers; Carleton's Stephanie Carvin said the likelier worry is hostile powers hiring people already inside Canada and noted counter-drone systems already protect nuclear plants — [Global News, 29 Sep](https://globalnews.ca/news/12077678/canadian-military-anti-drone-defences/)
- On 8 October a DND spokesperson called counter-drone capability "a critical consideration", referred to "unauthorized drone activity" that could threaten installations, declined specifics for operational security, and said parts of the procurement fall under a national security exception — [Global News, 8 Oct](https://globalnews.ca/news/12093248/dnd-counter-risks-of-drone-activity-at-bases/)
- Transport Canada's June newsletter confirms a temporary no-drone zone over all World Cup sites in Toronto and Vancouver and reports no incidents or fines — [Transport Canada, Zone de Drone no 8](https://tc.canada.ca/fr/aviation/securite-drones/zone-drone/zone-drone-numero-8-juin-2026)
- Toronto: seven drones seized by 17 June, including one near the Centennial Park training site; a security consultant described reconnaissance or perimeter bypass as the unlikely worst case — [CP24, 17 Jun](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/06/17/toronto-police-seize-seventh-drone-near-world-cup-events-as-authorities-crack-down/)
- Toronto: 20 drones seized by 29 June; police cited collision risk, flights over crowds and above 1,700 feet; no security threat mentioned — [CP24, 29 Jun](https://www.cp24.com/local/toronto/2026/06/29/toronto-police-seize-20-drones-near-fifa-venues-police-citing-safety-concerns/)
- Toronto police located a CBC test drone and its pilot near the stadium, declined to show detection hardware citing national security, and had charged both operators of two drones intercepted at the opening concert — [DroneXL, 12 Jun](https://dronexl.co/2026/06/12/toronto-police-tracked-down-cbc-rogue-test-drone/)
- A secondary outlet reports 17 people charged in Toronto as of 25 June under the Canadian Aviation Regulations; its own arithmetic is inconsistent and I could not confirm the figure — [SaharaReporters, 25 Jun](https://saharareporters.com/2026/06/25/17-persons-charged-canadian-authorities-flying-drones-around-world-cup-venues)
- Vancouver: 745 unauthorized drone flights on the seven match days, 45 operators identified and told to land, 40 drones jammed and removed, more than 800 police drone flights between 11 June and 13 July; no national security incident mentioned — [CP24 / Canadian Press, 20 Jul](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/07/20/vancouver-police-tell-of-drones-drunks-and-record-deployment-in-wake-of-world-cup/); [Noovo / La Presse Canadienne, 20 Jul](https://www.noovo.info/nouvelles/patrouille/article/la-police-de-vancouver-est-satisfaite-du-bilan-de-ses-operations-lors-du-mondial/)

### Inferences
- Counter-drone deployments, a national-security procurement exception and the phrase "unauthorized drone activity" suggest incidents may exist that are not public; this is inference only.
- The gap between Vancouver's 745 detected flights and 45 identified operators shows how much activity stays unattributed even with a full counter-drone posture; nothing indicates hostile intent, and repeated detections of the same aircraft are a likely contributor.

### Gaps
- No count, date or place of any drone overflight of a Canadian base in 2026 is public in the sources opened.
