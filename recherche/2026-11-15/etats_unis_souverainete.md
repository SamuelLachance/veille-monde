# United States-linked pressure, influence and security activity affecting Canadian sovereignty in 2026 (incl. Alberta referendum and Quebec election)

Research date: 2026-11-15. Method note (applies to every section): the session's shared WebSearch budget ran out after the first ~20 queries, so most verification was done by opening URLs surfaced in those searches, pages linked from them, Wikipedia articles (used as an index to primary reporting) and outlet topic pages. CBC, BBC, Politico, National Post, FT, Reuters, NYT, CNN and Maclean's could not be opened by the fetch tool; where a fact rests only on a headline/citation I saw (not the article body) this is flagged "(headline only)". Overt policy pressure is kept separate from covert/deniable activity throughout.

## 1. Annexation rhetoric and coercion in 2026 (51st state, tariffs, defence, China deal, Greenland/Venezuela, invasion modelling)

### Takeaway
Annexation talk and economic coercion were continuous and overt all year (map post and invasion-model leak in January, 100% tariff threat, bridge blockade threat, "51st State" posts, 50% tariffs and "fall in line" in August, "Lake America", "51st state ... just like Greenland" in October). Nothing I found shows a covert U.S. government operation behind these; the notable oddities are the documented private lobbying around the Gordie Howe bridge and the fact that both the military (January) and the Prime Minister (September) treated a U.S. attack as a risk worth planning for.

### Cited Findings
- 20 Jan 2026: the Globe and Mail reported (two unnamed senior officials) that the Canadian Armed Forces built a theoretical model of a U.S. invasion for the first time in over a century: U.S. forces could overcome Canadian positions on land and sea in as little as two days; response based on insurgency-style ambushes and hit-and-run tactics; described as "a conceptual and theoretical framework, not a military plan"; warning sign would be the U.S. ending NORAD cooperation; Canada would likely call on Britain and France; officials judged an invasion unlikely. The military did not immediately comment — [The Defense Post/AFP](https://thedefensepost.com/2026/01/21/canada-military-us-invasion/amp/); also [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-20/canada-s-military-has-modeled-hypothetical-us-invasion-reports-say) (headline only)
- Overnight 19-20 Jan 2026 Trump posted an apparently AI-generated map showing the U.S. flag over the U.S., Canada, Greenland and Venezuela; on 21 Jan at Davos he said Canada "lives because of the United States"; NBC reported the weekend before that Trump privately complained about Canada's Arctic "vulnerability" — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11619858/donald-trump-greenland-canada-arctic-norad/)

Wikipedia also records that Canada considered sending troops to Greenland (reported 19 Jan), announced a consulate in Nuuk (6 Feb) and joined NATO's Arctic Sentry (11 Feb) — [Wikipedia: Greenland crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_crisis) (Canada details not checked against primary sources)

No implementation reported — [AP via ABC7](https://abc7news.com/post/president-donald-trump-threatens-canada-100-tariff-china-trade-deal-escalates-feud-prime-minister-mark-carney/18465052/)

### Inferences
- All of this is overt state pressure (suspicion 1 on the rubric). The single element with a deniable/opaque dimension is the bridge episode, where a private competitor's donations and access preceded a presidential threat and Canada ended up conceding toll revenue.
- The January leak of the CAF model and the PM's September confirmation show that Ottawa itself treats U.S. coercion beyond trade as a planning scenario; that is an official-response data point, not evidence of U.S. intent.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the 6.1%/49,000-vehicle terms of the Canada–China arrangement, any Golden Dome-specific threat in 2026, or an April 2026 "Cherished 51st State" post (seen only in search snippets, e.g. [Al Bawaba](https://www.albawaba.net/news/trump-renews-call-canada-become-1626081)).
- No 2026 reporting was found on Machias Seal Island or Northwest Passage incidents (the one hit, Eurasian Times, is a 2018 article).
- Canada's reaction to the 3 Jan 2026 Venezuela operation was not verified.

## 2. Alberta separatism: U.S. contacts, influence networks and the 19 October 2026 referendum

### Takeaway
Three State Department meetings with Alberta Prosperity Project (APP) leaders in 2025 are acknowledged by Washington ("staff-level", "no commitments"); a Globe and Mail investigation (3 Oct 2026) added that Michigan Republican-linked operatives pitched voter-targeting software to separatists in Calgary and that a separatist document offered the U.S. water and oil royalties. Researchers attribute most covert online amplification to Russia-linked networks, with a smaller, overt U.S. influencer share; no Canadian agency has publicly reported state interference, and the referendum failed 74.9% to 25.1%.

## 3. Quebec: foreign influence around the sovereignty movement and the 5 October 2026 election

### Takeaway
The Quebec campaign featured one concrete U.S.-origin document (a pseudonymous think-tank essay urging Washington to fragment Canada and back the PQ), a leak-driven interference scare on 25 September that the premier herself walked back, confirmed U.S. embassy approaches to Quebec on tariffs, and an open intervention by Commerce Secretary Lutnick. No authority reported actual interference; the PQ won a minority and ruled out a referendum while Trump is in office.

### Cited Findings
- 26 Feb 2026: the Defense Analyses and Research Corporation (DARC; Arlington VA, El Segundo CA, London) published "The Centrifugal Strategy: Fragmentation as strategy in the northern Monroe Doctrine" by pseudonymous "Mukden": "U.S. strategy should be to achieve the fragmentation, not annexation, of Canada"; recommends support for the PQ before the October election, psychological operations to stoke Quebec and Alberta separatism, private trade talks offering Quebec lower tariffs, and covertly bankrolling Indigenous land lawsuits; no government affiliation stated — [DARC](https://defenseanalyses.org/work/the-centrifugal-strategy/)
- 27 Aug 2026 (campaign day 1): Lutnick said in Washington that Ottawa invented U.S. demands on French: "c'était Mark Carney tentant de fabriquer quelque chose qui les empêcherait de gagner" (referring to the PQ); Fréchette said she believed Carney and called Trump "le véritable adversaire" — [Noovo Info/La Presse Canadienne](https://www.noovo.info/nouvelles/politique/article/frechette-croit-carney-et-discredite-lutnick/). National Post headline: "Lutnick claims Canada pulled out of trade talks due to the Quebec election, Alberta referendum" — [National Post](https://nationalpost.com/news/leblanc-welcomes-u-s-comments-on-french-language-protections-raises-possibility-of-renewed-trade-talks) (headline only)
- 18-19 Aug 2026: PQ leader St-Pierre Plamondon said no referendum while Trump is president (not before January 2029): the "unpredictability of the American administration will harm our process" — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/19/quebec-separatist-says-no-independence-referendum-while-trump-in-office)
- 25 Sept 2026: Québecor outlets reported Ottawa had warned Quebec of possible U.S. interference and that a U.S. think tank identified the PQ as the party to "support"; two Quebec government sources said the U.S. embassy had contacted the Fréchette government in recent months; Fréchette was informed of the DARC writings on 19 Sept, met CSIS, wrote to Carney, and said nothing confirmed interference in the electoral process; PSPP said the PQ was never contacted and found the timing odd — [Noovo Info](https://www.noovo.info/nouvelles/politique/elections-quebec/article/guerre-commerciale-frechette-se-plaint-a-carney-dallegations-dingerence-americaine-dans-les-elections-quebecoises/)
- Same day: CAQ contacted CSIS on 22 Sept and met it on 23 Sept; Fréchette confirmed American officials approached her government in the spring about CUSMA-related sectors and that she rejected the tariff idea "right away"; Foreign Minister Anand: "Right now I do not have proof"; PSPP said the information had reportedly been available since January — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12073854/quebec-election-u-s-interference/)
- 26 Sept: Liberal leader Milliard raised questions about PSPP meetings with U.S. officials; PSPP said he met only representatives of other countries and demanded a retraction; Duhaime played down the risk — [Noovo/La Presse Canadienne](https://www.noovo.info/nouvelles/politique/elections-quebec/article/jour-31-affrontements-sur-lingerence-constitution-quebecoise-et-immigration/)
- 1 Oct: a senior Quebec official had received the DARC writings from Ottawa in early September; Fréchette admitted a member of her team leaked the information and said "J'aurais dû attendre d'avoir plus d'informations" — [Noovo/La Presse Canadienne](https://www.noovo.info/nouvelles/politique/elections-quebec/article/ingerence-etrangere-frechette-admet-quelle-aurait-du-attendre-avant-de-reagir/)
- 5 Oct 2026 result: PQ minority (59 seats on election night per CP; Wikipedia lists 60), PLQ 40/39, PCQ 19, QS 9, CAQ 0; PQ 28.0% of the vote — [CP24/Canadian Press](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/10/06/leaders-react-as-parti-quebecois-forms-minority-government/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Quebec_general_election)

### Inferences
- The DARC essay is real and explicit, but it is advocacy by an anonymous author at a small think tank; there is no public evidence the U.S. government adopted it. Its tariff-carve-out idea does resemble the embassy approach Fréchette says she rejected, which is the detail worth watching.
- The 25 September scare was driven by a partisan leak ten days before the vote and was walked back within hours; it should be scored as an unverified alert, not as interference.
- Lutnick's remarks are overt, on-the-record commentary by a U.S. cabinet member on a provincial election.

### Gaps
- I did not find the original Journal de Montréal/TVA reports, any public CSIS conclusion, an Élections Québec statement, or any U.S. embassy response.
- Who "Mukden" is, who funds DARC, and whether U.S. officials read or used the essay are unknown.
- No post-election reporting on foreign influence was found (CTV lists only "Many Quebec voters got false election information from chatbot, MPs hear" — [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/montreal/article/many-quebec-voters-got-false-election-information-from-chatbot-mps-hear/), headline only).

## 4. U.S. intelligence and law-enforcement activity affecting Canada in 2026

### Takeaway
I found no 2026 reporting of U.S. espionage against Canada or of U.S. agents operating unlawfully on Canadian soil. What is documented is overt: the FBI-led capture of Canadian fugitive Ryan Wedding in Mexico, U.S. Treasury sanctions on Canadian money-services firms, ICE/CBP detentions of Canadians and friction over First Nations border-crossing rights, and a Canadian budget move to cut dependence on allied (U.S.) intelligence.

## 7. Canadian official and research assessments in 2026 naming U.S.-origin interference or disinformation

### Takeaway
No Canadian government body publicly attributed interference to the United States in 2026: CSIS's annual report named China, India, Russia, Iran and Pakistan, and its director said overt U.S. commentary falls outside the legal definition. The naming of U.S.-origin influence comes from researchers (DisinfoWatch/Cipher, University of Regina, Media Ecosystem Observatory) and from politicians' remarks.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- There is a structural blind spot: the legal definition of foreign interference excludes overt influence, which is precisely the U.S. mode in 2026, so official silence on the U.S. is not evidence of absence of influence.
- Research findings consistently describe U.S. activity as overt and influencer-driven and Russian activity as covert; that asymmetry should drive scoring.

### Gaps
- No NSICOP, PCO or SITE document addressing U.S. activity or the Alberta referendum was found.
- The full text of the CSIS public report was not read; "U.S. not mentioned" relies on Global News coverage.
