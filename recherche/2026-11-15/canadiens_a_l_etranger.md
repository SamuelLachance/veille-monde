## 1. Canadians arrested, charged or accused abroad of espionage or national-security offences; arbitrary detention, exit bans, executions and "hostage diplomacy"

### Takeaway
The NATO lead is real: Belgian federal prosecutors announced on 25 July 2026 the arrest of a Canadian woman interning at SHAPE (Mons) on charges of espionage for an unnamed third country and participation in a criminal organisation; she is still in pre-trial detention and her identity, the beneficiary state and her vetting history remain undisclosed. No new espionage-type detention of a Canadian in China, Russia, Iran, Venezuela, India or Algeria could be verified; the notable detention-related developments are the reversal of Robert Schellenberg's death sentence, Canadians trapped in wartime Kuwait by employer exit permits, a 60% rise in U.S. arrests/detentions, and a Canadian wanted by the RCMP whom Ottawa will not repatriate from Türkiye.

### Cited Findings
- NATO intern, arrest: Belgian prosecutors announced on Saturday 25 July 2026 the arrest of a Canadian national of Chinese origin (a woman, per the video caption) who was an intern at Supreme Headquarters Allied Powers Europe in Mons; charges are espionage and participation in a criminal organisation; SHAPE's own security services noticed her and reported the matter to Belgium's military intelligence agency — [Canadian Press via Global News, 27 Jul 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11996006/canadian-nato-intern-spying-minister/)
- NATO intern, ministers: Public Safety Minister Gary Anandasangaree (27 July, New Westminster) called the allegations quite serious, promised to get to the bottom of the matter, said officials would examine whether she had done any work for agencies in Canada, and said such incidents must not recur — [Canadian Press via Global News, 27 Jul 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11996006/canadian-nato-intern-spying-minister/)

### Inferences
- In the NATO case the two official Canadian statements pull in different directions: the minister's 27 July language reads as surprise and a promise to investigate, while the Prime Minister's 5 August line implies Canadian services were in the loop before or at the arrest. Either Canadian counter-intelligence helped build the case, or "from the start" only means from the day of the arrest. The Belgian account credits SHAPE security and Belgian military intelligence and does not mention Canada.
- The "criminal organisation" count implies at least one other participant; none has been publicly identified.
- The beneficiary state is officially unnamed. The prosecutor's unusual mention of her origin and Reuters' China filing are suggestive but are not an attribution.
- Schellenberg's trajectory mirrors the bilateral relationship in both directions (escalation to death weeks after the Meng arrest; reversal weeks after the Carney–Xi reset), which supports the long-standing reading that the case is politically indexed, though no official has said so.

### Gaps
- No Belgian-language source, federal prosecutor communiqué or NATO statement could be opened; nothing after 5 August was found on the intern case (next monthly detention hearings, any transfer, any Canadian review).
- No verified 2026 case of a Canadian newly detained for espionage or national security in China, Russia, Iran, India, Algeria, Venezuela or the Gulf; Kovrig's figure of about 100 Canadians held in China is an estimate, not a list.
- Identity and circumstances of the Canadian killed in Iran remain unknown; no follow-up found.
- Outcome of the Warner detention, of the Kuwait exit-permit cases and of Boughadou's Federal Court application not found.
- Mexican government statements on the Wedding arrest, and any RCMP role, were not verified (AP, BBC, CNN, CBC blocked).

## 2. Deaths, disappearances or kidnappings of Canadians abroad under suspicious circumstances; Canadians killed in conflicts or strikes

### Takeaway
Verified 2026 deaths with a state or conflict nexus: one Canadian killed by Iranian authorities (January), one Windsor man killed in Lebanon by what his family says was an Israeli drone (10 April; Ottawa has demanded an Israeli investigation), two British Columbians killed by drones in eastern Ukraine (18 August and late October), and one Canadian gunner who died near Riga in January with no cause published. No Canadian death was found in the Iran war itself, in U.S. boat strikes or in the Venezuela operation.

### Cited Findings

- Ukraine, volunteer: Jesse Adams, founder of Victoria's RainCoast Dog Rescue Society, died after an FPV drone hit the vehicle he was using to move animals in eastern Ukraine; the rescue society reported it; no Global Affairs statement in the article — [Global News, 27 Oct 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/12123428/bc-man-jesse-adams-raincoast-dog-rescue-killed-saving-animals-ukraine/)

## 3. Hostile acts against Canadian missions and forces abroad (embassies, anomalous health incidents, Latvia, Ukraine training, Indo-Pacific, Gulf)

### Takeaway
The clearest hostile act against Canadian forces in 2026 was Iran's missile strike of about 1 March on Ali Al-Salem Air Base in Kuwait, which damaged the Canadian camp and which Ottawa did not disclose until La Presse reported it on 12 March. Canadians were also embedded in U.S. headquarters in Qatar and Bahrain that Iran hit. In the Indo-Pacific the record is political rather than kinetic: HMCS Charlottetown transited the Taiwan Strait alone on 22–23 May after China's ambassador had warned against further transits. No 2026 unsafe intercept, embassy attack, sabotage or spying incident against the Latvia brigade was found.

### Cited Findings
- Kuwait strike: the Canadian camp at Ali Al-Salem Air Base (Canada's operational support hub, shared with the U.S. Air Force) was hit in an Iranian missile attack on 1 March according to Conservative critic James Bezan; La Presse reported on 12 March that it appeared damaged; no Canadian was injured; DND took about a week to answer, cited operational security, acknowledged only reports of strikes near the base, and said it had relocated some personnel and sent some home; Carney said the troops were safe but did not address the attack; the 9 March take-note debate did not mention it — [Canadian Press via Global News, 12 Mar 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11728266/iran-kuwait-military-base-canada-attack/)
- Kuwait strike, other coverage: CTV framed it as Ottawa being questioned over non-disclosure — [CTV News, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.ctvnews.ca/politics/article/feds-face-questions-on-why-strike-on-kuwait-military-hub-was-not-made-public/); the Western Standard reported Canadian personnel safe and unharmed — [Western Standard, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.westernstandard.news/news/canadian-military-instillation-in-kuwait-hit-by-iranian-missiles/71845)
- Date conflict: Wikipedia places the Camp Canada strike on 2 March and mentions satellite imagery of damaged bunkers; Bezan says 1 March; DND refers to events since 27 February — [Wikipedia: Canada and the 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_and_the_2026_Iran_war)
- Qatar and Bahrain: per CBC as summarised by Wikipedia, members of 1 Canadian Air Division were at the Combined Aerospace Operations Centre at Al Udeid when Iran struck it on 3 March and Canadians worked at U.S. Fifth Fleet headquarters in Bahrain when it was hit; DND's site lists up to 18 members under Operation FOUNDATION; retired Maj.-Gen. Denis Thompson said Canadian staff officers would likely be directly involved in targeting, which DND denied — [Wikipedia: Canada and the 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_and_the_2026_Iran_war); underlying CBC article, URL seen, not opened: [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/politics/iran-air-strikes-canada-israel-war-9.7110268)
- McGuinty declined to give troop numbers, said only that some personnel were in the region, that the CAF neither prepared nor carried out the strikes and that Canada had no advance notice — [Canadian Press via Global News, 3 Mar 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11714341/iran-canadian-troops-middle-east-mcguinty/)
- Iranian accusations: on 17 August Iran's foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei claimed on X that Canada had joined the U.S.-led war to please Washington; Canadian officials said Canada was neither consulted nor involved in offensive operations — [Anadolu, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/iran-slams-canadas-participation-in-us-war-on-iran/4029034); he repeated the charge on 8 and 24 September — [Wikipedia: Canada–Iran relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93Iran_relations)
- Background: Iran designated the Royal Canadian Navy a terrorist organisation in late December 2025 in reply to Canada's IRGC listing — [Wikipedia: Canada–Iran relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93Iran_relations) (Al Arabiya, 30 Dec 2025, URL seen: [link](https://english.alarabiya.net/News/middle-east/2025/12/30/iran-designates-royal-canadian-navy-a-terrorist-organization))
- Consular operations in the Gulf: Global Affairs confirmed on 27 May that it helped citizens of partner countries including the U.S., U.K. and Japan, coordinated at Iran's border crossings with Five Eyes partners plus France and Japan, after Semafor reported that Canada's embassy in the UAE gave U.S. consular staff counter space to hand out passports — [Global News, 27 May 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11870530/canada-assisted-americans-fleeing-iran/); Semafor original, URL seen: [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/05/26/2026/the-us-relied-on-a-spurned-friend-during-the-iran-war-canada)

- Deployment: Charlottetown left Halifax around 31 January for six months under Operations HORIZON and NEON with about 240 personnel and a CH-148 Cyclone — [Global News, 1 Feb 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11647548/canadian-warship-hmcs-charlottetown-indo-pacific-mission/)
- Operation NEON: the official page (modified 29 April 2026) lists no 2026 deployment and says the mandate runs until April 2026; it mentions no interactions with Chinese forces — [National Defence](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/services/operations/military-operations/current-operations/operation-neon.html)
- Havana syndrome: on 20 March 2026 Ottawa said it stands by Global Affairs' 2024 report (no malicious foreign act; pre-existing conditions and environmental factors likely) even as U.S. intelligence and law-enforcement chiefs told Congress that similar assessments should be retracted as flawed and 60 Minutes alleged the U.S. had tested a Russian-built pulsed-microwave device; eight former Canadian diplomats said they were outraged; their seven-year lawsuit continues with the latest claim under seal — [Global News, 24 Mar 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11742192/havana-syndrome-canada-diplomats-reaction/); the 20 March item on Ottawa standing by the report was seen as a headline only: [Global News, 20 Mar 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11739481/havana-syndrome-canada-report-response/); U.S. developments (device purchase, 60 Minutes report of March 2026) are also summarised on [Wikipedia: Havana syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havana_syndrome)
- Latvia theatre context: drones entering from Russia crashed at Rēzekne on 7 May (the defence minister resigned on 10 May); NATO jets shot down drones over Latvia on 8 June and 14 August; no Canadian involvement is recorded — [Wikipedia: 2026 in Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_in_Latvia)
- CSE says it supported Operations UNIFIER, REASSURANCE and HORIZON with early warning, counter-intelligence on threats to personnel and analysis of adversary electronic warfare, and deployed Cyber Centre analysts to Latvia and Lithuania — [CSE Annual Report 2025-2026](https://www.cse-cst.gc.ca/en/accountability/transparency/reports/communications-security-establishment-canada-annual-report-2025-2026)
- DND on drones: counter-drone capability called critical; personnel deployed in Europe already have anti-drone protection; no incidents, places or numbers disclosed — [Global News, 8 Oct 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/12093248/dnd-counter-risks-of-drone-activity-at-bases/)

### Inferences
- The Kuwait episode is less about the strike (the base is a U.S. hub and Canadians were probably not the intended target) than about eleven days of official silence, a House debate that omitted it, and a DND statement that neither confirms nor denies damage. That pattern, plus the embedded officers in Qatar and Bahrain, gave Tehran material for its later claim that Canada is a participant.
- The Charlottetown transit, made alone and within a month of an explicit ambassadorial warning and days before Wang Yi's visit, reads as a deliberate signal that the trade reset did not buy a change in naval practice. That is an inference; DND called it routine.
- Ottawa's refusal to revisit its Havana report while Washington reverses course coincides with its position as defendant in the diplomats' lawsuit.

### Gaps
- No 2026 report of unsafe intercepts of RCAF aircraft or shadowing of RCN ships by Chinese forces was found; whether Operation NEON was renewed past April 2026 is not stated on the official page.
- No sabotage, espionage, disinformation or GPS-jamming incident specifically targeting the Canadian-led brigade in Latvia was found; Wikipedia's Operation Reassurance and Operation Unifier pages have no 2026 operational entries.
- La Presse's original report on the Kuwait damage, and the extent of that damage, were not seen.
- No attack on or surveillance of a Canadian embassy in 2026 was found.

## 4. Canadian intelligence or special-forces activity abroad revealed, alleged or criticised (CSIS, CSE, CANSOFCOM; NSIRA, NSICOP, courts; foreign accusations against Canada)

### Takeaway
2026 produced official disclosure and policy movement rather than exposed operations: CSE reported three active and one defensive foreign cyber-operation authorisations and action against ten ransomware groups; NSICOP opened a review of foreign-intelligence collection (11 June); and the 5 November budget gave CSIS $6 billion over ten years amid open speculation about a foreign human-intelligence capability. Two foreign governments publicly accused Canada: Iran of joining the war, and the U.S. embassy of political "influence" before the midterms. No JTF2/CANSOFCOM deployment was revealed in the sources reached.

### Cited Findings
- CSE: 13 ministerial authorisations in 2025-26, four for foreign cyber operations (three active, one defensive); concurrent action against ten major ransomware groups, one of which (behind more than 25 incidents in Canada) had its infrastructure rendered inoperable and stolen data deleted; operations also against a phishing campaign, foreign-based fentanyl precursor brokers and a violent extremist group; ministerial orders designate the government systems of Latvia, Ukraine and Lithuania as of importance; 200 operational compliance incidents, 186 involving information about Canadians; page dated 29 June 2026 — [CSE Annual Report 2025-2026](https://www.cse-cst.gc.ca/en/accountability/transparency/reports/communications-security-establishment-canada-annual-report-2025-2026)
- NSICOP: on 11 June 2026 the committee launched a review of Canada's framework for collecting foreign intelligence, to assess effectiveness within current authorities and whether and where gaps exist; no agencies or timeline named — [NSICOP statement, 11 Jun 2026](https://www.canada.ca/en/national-security-intelligence-committee-parliamentarians/services/statement-2026-06.html)
- Context for that review: CSIS may collect foreign intelligence only within Canada; chair Darren Fisher declined an interview; access-to-information records show ministers were briefed on expanding foreign-intelligence capacity; a 2025 CSIS memo notes historic reliance on allies — [Canadian Press via Global News, 20 Sep 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/12066144/canada-foreign-spy-agency-debate/)
- Budget: $6 billion over a decade for CSIS, the largest injection in its history, with almost no detail; experts Stephanie Carvin and Wesley Wark read it with the new National Security Strategy as a foreign HUMINT capability in the making; Privy Council official Dominic Rochon suggested CSIS Act changes are planned — [Global News, 6 Nov 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/12140578/csis-budget-boost-foreign-intelligence/)
- NSIRA 2026 output relevant to foreign activity: DND/CAF open-source intelligence review published 29 October 2026 (PDF only, not read); CSE–CSIS operational collaboration review posted 2 February 2026; a review of overseas missions (CBSA, CSIS, DND/CAF, GAC, IRCC, RCMP) remains in progress; a CSIS warranted threat-reduction-measure review was submitted 30 April 2026 — [NSIRA: Find a review](https://nsira-ossnr.gc.ca/en/reviews/find-a-review/)
- NSIRA's 2024 annual report (released under access to information) found CSIS had not formally reported 22 admitted instances of Charter non-compliance in 2023-24 to the minister; no overseas dimension stated — [Global News, 29 May 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11874149/spy-agency-didnt-report-unlawful-activity-watchdog/)
- U.S. accusation: on 21 October the U.S. embassy in Ottawa under Ambassador Hoekstra published nine "facts" said to show Canadian politicians trying to influence American politics (mostly Mélanie Joly's U.S. trips and Ontario's anti-tariff advertising) and told Canadians to refrain from influencing U.S. elections; Joly called it ordinary diplomacy with both parties; legal scholar Leah West said the examples do not meet the covert-or-clandestine threshold; no reprisals reported — [Global News, 22 Oct 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/12113849/diplomacy-foreign-interference-us-embassy-midterms/)
- Iranian accusation of participation in the war: see section 3 — [Anadolu, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/iran-slams-canadas-participation-in-us-war-on-iran/4029034)
- Canadian counter-intelligence member and Ukraine (domestic proceeding, cross-reference only): Master Warrant Officer Matthew Robar of Canadian Forces Intelligence Command, accused of passing operational information to Ukrainian contacts (CBC reported a 2024 meeting with a Ukrainian representative in Lithuania), faces a court martial set for 9 December 2026 — [Kyiv Independent, 5 Nov 2026](https://kyivindependent.com/canadian-service-member-to-face-court-martial-for-sharing-sensitive-information-with-ukraine/)

### Inferences
- The sequence NSICOP review (June) → National Security Strategy → CSIS budget (November) → hinted CSIS Act amendments points to a decision in principle to build foreign HUMINT inside CSIS; nothing official says so yet.
- The U.S. embassy list is best read as domestic U.S. political messaging; it nonetheless creates a public record that could later be cited to justify measures against Canadian officials.

### Gaps
- No public 2026 revelation of a CSIS foreign station, CANSOFCOM/JTF2 deployment or evacuation operation was found (Global News' JTF2 tag has nothing after 2023; Ottawa Citizen's Defence Watch could not be opened).
- NSIRA's 24-10 report text and the CSE–CSIS collaboration findings were not read.
- NSICOP's review scope, the agencies covered and any reporting date are undisclosed.

## 5. Canadians or Canadian companies allegedly working for foreign services, sanctioned or charged abroad; Canadian technology in foreign weapons; foreign fighters; passport misuse

### Takeaway
Three sanction actions with a strong Canadian nexus were verified: Canada's own August listing of Streit Group (an armoured-vehicle maker founded and run by a Canadian citizen, based in the UAE, accused of supplying Russia's National Guard); U.S. Treasury's 20 March designation of a Hezbollah finance network whose leader holds a Canadian passport and which includes a B.C.-registered company; and Treasury's 6 November designation of eight Canadian money-services businesses tied to Iran's crypto sector and to Ryan Wedding. A Chinese student at the University of Waterloo is charged in Michigan after allegedly photographing airport cargo facilities in Chicago and Toronto for a contact he believed was tied to the Chinese Communist Party.

- Canada listed Streit roughly ten months after the EU and cannot list its Canadian owner; the absence of any reported prosecution after years of UN, U.S. and EU findings is the oddity.

- The Zeng tasking (low pay per photo, SIM cards, a non-existent forum) fits the pattern of low-cost test tasking described by a former RCMP officer in the article, but he is charged only with false statements and the Chinese-state link rests on his own alleged belief.
