- An OCCRP/Delfi investigation, as summarized by DisinfoWatch on 11 November 2026, found the hacked archive of the Doppelgänger outlet RRN holds interviews with 14 people with Canadian connections among more than 500 interviews from over 50 countries; several interviewees said they did not know where the interviews would appear; DisinfoWatch notes that being interviewed does not prove knowing participation — [DisinfoWatch](https://disinfowatch.org/14-canadians-interviewed-for-russias-doppelganger-propaganda-network/)

### Inferences
- No Canadian government body publicly attributed a specific 2026 disinformation operation against Canada to Russia; the attribution chain runs through NGOs, a university lab with a commercial arm, and one premier's account of classified briefings.
- Russian activity looks opportunistic and amplifying (Pravda aggregation, RT social accounts) rather than originating; in the two cases with the most reach (Wainwright rumours, fake Toronto Sun) Russian authorship is unproven.

## 7. Official Canadian statements in 2026 about Russian hybrid threats (CSIS, CSE, DND/CAF, NORAD, Global Affairs sanctions)

### Takeaway
Official 2026 messaging is consistent and general: CSIS calls Russian activity hybrid warfare and a direct threat; the Senate calls Russian disinformation urgent; Global Affairs issued at least seven Russia sanctions amendments, some labelled hybrid, disinformation or cyber, without naming those targets; DND and the foreign minister stress Arctic demonstration of sovereignty. None of these statements discloses a specific Russian operation on Canadian soil.

### Cited Findings
- 24 February 2026: sanctions on 21 individuals, 53 entities and 100 shadow-fleet vessels targeting energy revenues, financial enablers (including cryptocurrency infrastructure) and "conventional and hybrid military capabilities" including AI and drone production; oil price cap lowered to US$44.10 — [National Defence / Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/news/2026/02/government-of-canada-reaffirms-unwavering-support-for-ukraine-four-years-into-russias-full-scale-invasion.html)
- 26 March 2026: 100 more shadow-fleet vessels; more than 600 vessels sanctioned to date — [Global Affairs Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/global-affairs/news/2026/03/minister-anand-announces-additional-sanctions-against-russia.html)
- 16 June 2026 (Évian G7): 162 individuals, entities and vessels, including "disinformation actors" — [Prime Minister's Office](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/readouts/2026/06/16/prime-minister-carney-meets-president-ukraine-volodymyr-zelenskyy); the Global Affairs summary of the 12 June amendment (7 individuals, 34 entities, 121 vessels) does not mention disinformation — [Global Affairs Canada sanctions page](https://www.international.gc.ca/world-monde/international_relations-relations_internationales/sanctions/russia-russie.aspx?lang=eng)
- The June amendments also repealed the minister's obligation to decide delisting applications within 90 days — [Baker McKenzie](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/canada-expands-russia-sanctions-at-g7-summit/)
- 9 November 2026 (Paris, G7 foreign ministers): 24 individuals and 38 entities tied to the defence industrial base including ballistic missile technology, "Russia's cyber activities" and financial enablers such as crypto exchanges; no names in the release — [Global Affairs Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/global-affairs/news/2026/11/minister-anand-announces-additional-sanctions-to-reduce-russias-missile-capabilities-and-raise-economic-costs.html); [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/canada-unveils-new-russia-sanctions-targeting-defense-industry-cyber-activities/)
- Other 2026 amendments: 8 May (23 individuals, 5 entities) and 4 September (8 individuals) over the deportation of Ukrainian children — [Global Affairs Canada sanctions page](https://www.international.gc.ca/world-monde/international_relations-relations_internationales/sanctions/russia-russie.aspx?lang=eng)
- 4 March 2026: Anand told the Ottawa Conference on Defence and Security that NATO must also look northward and cited Russian infrastructure moving further north — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11715875/nato-northward-arctic-threats-anand/)
- Outgoing ambassador to Ukraine Natalka Cmoc reportedly said (Ukrinform interview, 1 July 2026) that Canada started an analysts' academy with partners and a fund for victims of hybrid attacks; secondary source only — [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/07/02/canada-launches-new-measures-as-russian-hybrid-threats-deepen/)
- CSE says it informed efforts to counter a Russian campaign promoting its narrative on the war in Ukraine and holds three active and one defensive cyber operation authorizations — [CSE](https://www.cse-cst.gc.ca/en/accountability/transparency/reports/communications-security-establishment-canada-annual-report-2025-2026)

### Inferences
- The gap between strong official language ("hybrid warfare", "urgent threat") and the absence of named Canadian cases is itself the most consistent pattern in the 2026 record; the Senate committee's own call for more transparency points the same way.
- The Prime Minister's Office and Global Affairs describe the June package differently (disinformation named by one, not the other), a small inconsistency worth checking against the regulation text.

### Gaps
- The regulation texts (Canada Gazette) were not opened, so the identities of "hybrid", "disinformation" and "cyber" designees remain unverified.
- No 2026 Canadian sanctions announcement explicitly tied to sabotage was found.
- No standalone 2026 DND/CAF statement on Russian hybrid threats to Canada (as opposed to Europe) was found.
