## 2. What happened in 2026 in Cameron Ortis's appeal, the Quebec "militia" case, and the Scoretz, Dennis Lu and NATO-intern cases?

### Takeaway
Ortis's appeal is still pending but he has been on day parole since February 2026 and now concedes he lacked authority to share the material; the Quebec militia case produced unsealed documents, an unexplained blanket publication ban, a jury trial fixed for September 2027 and a guilty plea by the fourth accused for trafficking U.S.-controlled night-vision gear. Nothing new was found for Scoretz or the NATO intern after the indexed entries, and the Dennis Lu case added one contradiction: CSIS now says the department tipped it off, not the reverse.

## 4. Which 2026 NSICOP, NSIRA, Intelligence Commissioner, Auditor General or Federal Court publications criticised CSIS, CSE, the RCMP or CBSA, beyond those already recorded?

### Takeaway
NSIRA published at least six further reviews in 2026 that the first pass did not record, three of them finding actual or possible non-compliance with the law by CSE and CSIS, and the Intelligence Commissioner's 2025 annual report discloses his refusal of a new class of otherwise-illegal acts sought for CSIS. Nothing new was found from NSICOP beyond the indexed items, and no critical Auditor General or Federal Court publication on these agencies could be located.

### Cited Findings

**NSIRA (dates are the "Date of Publishing" shown on each report page)**

## 5. What is the 2026 status of the transnational-repression bill (C-219), lawful access (C-22) and related powers, and of the Hogue recommendations as of November 2026?

### Takeaway
C-219 passed the Commons on 8 October 2026 and is now before the Senate foreign-affairs committee; C-22 is in Senate committee (already indexed); and an elections-integrity bill the first pass missed, C-25, received royal assent on 18 June 2026. No official November progress report on the Hogue recommendations was found: the government's own count (34 of 51 advanced, February) contrasts with an independent expert's count (C-25 enacts six; steps taken on eight of the 24 priority recommendations, April).

### Cited Findings
- C-219 (James Bezan; "Sergei Magnitsky Law No. 3"): second reading 24 February 2026; six committee meetings 30 April–16 June; reported with amendments 18 June; report stage and third reading 8 October 2026 with no recorded division; Senate first reading 20 October; second reading and referral to the Senate Foreign Affairs and International Trade committee 3 November; no committee activity listed yet — [LEGISinfo C-219](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-219); [LEGISinfo (français)](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/fr/projet-de-loi/45-1/c-219)
- Summary of the bill as passed by the House: new duties for the Minister of Foreign Affairs on human rights, prisoners of conscience and human-rights defenders; transnational repression added as a ground for sanctions under the Magnitsky law and the Special Economic Measures Act (to be renamed); tabling of orders in Parliament; higher penalties — [Bill C-219, third-reading text](https://www.parl.ca/DocumentViewer/en/45-1/bill/C-219/third-reading)
- C-22 (Lawful Access Act, 2026): Senate second reading and referral to the National Security, Defence and Veterans Affairs committee on 22 October; committee meeting on 2 November; no report yet — [LEGISinfo C-22](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-22). The Intelligence Commissioner told senators the bill should make proportionality and reasonable alternatives explicit factors — [Intelligence Commissioner, 2026-11-02](https://www.canada.ca/en/intelligence-commissioner/appearancebeforesecdnov22026.html) (already reflected in the index entry of 12 March).
- C-25 (Strong and Free Elections Act): first reading 26 March 2026; second reading 24 April; reported with amendments 3 June; third reading 11 June; Senate first reading 15 June, third reading 18 June; royal assent 18 June 2026 (S.C. 2026, c. 20) — [LEGISinfo C-25](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-25)
- Content of C-25: new and revised prohibitions on undue foreign influence, bribery, unauthorised computer use and false or misleading information about elections and nominations; the same prohibitions extended to nomination and leadership contests; bans on anonymous and cryptoasset contributions; impersonation offence extended to images and voice (deepfakes) with a parody exception; some offences apply outside Canada; higher penalties and new powers for the Commissioner of Canada Elections — [Bill C-25, royal assent text](https://www.parl.ca/DocumentViewer/en/45-1/bill/C-25/royal-assent)
- Expert assessment (Wesley Wark, The Hub 21 April, CIGI 27 April 2026): C-25 enacts six of Hogue's 51 recommendations (31, 40, 41, 42, 43, 49); of the 24 the commissioner wanted adopted early, steps have been taken on eight; a revised cabinet directive ("Coordinated Response to Threats to Elections") replaces the 2019 public protocol, adds a sixth panel member and drops the independent-review requirement; no public warning was issued in the 2019, 2021 or 2025 elections; still missing are an open-source intelligence capacity, a public mandate for the national security adviser, a published foreign-interference strategy and reform of the SITE task force — [CIGI (Substack copy), 2026-04-27](https://cigionlineorg.substack.com/p/ottawas-election-security-fix-is)

### Inferences
- The legislative response to the Hogue report in 2026 is concentrated in election law (C-25) and the influence registry (in force 4 August, indexed); the institutional recommendations remain largely unverified from public sources.
- C-219 would be the first statute to name transnational repression in sanctions law, and it comes from an opposition private member, not the government.

### Gaps
- No government progress report on the Hogue recommendations dated after February 2026 was found; the one-year report to Parliament the commissioner asked for could not be located.
- The amendments made to C-219 in committee and at report stage were not examined.
- The French short title of C-25 shown by LEGISinfo was not cross-checked against the statute.
