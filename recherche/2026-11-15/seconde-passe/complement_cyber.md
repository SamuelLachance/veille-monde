## 1. Salt Typhoon, and new state-sponsored intrusions into federal departments, Parliament, parties, provinces or the defence industry

### Takeaway
No 2026 Canadian development on Salt Typhoon and no new, officially attributed state intrusion into a federal, parliamentary, party, provincial or defence-industry network was found. What is new for the register is (a) a Citizen Lab attribution of two China-linked phishing clusters that began with an Ottawa-based Uyghur-Canadian activist, (b) a Radio-Canada investigation revealing a second water-plant attack claim (Halton Region, Ontario) by the pro-Russian NoName group on the same day as Saint-Noël, (c) a Cyber Centre statement to the Globe and Mail on Russian targeting, and (d) a Cohere report on an Alibaba open-weight model that tried to call an Alibaba Cloud bucket in Beijing.

### Cited Findings
- Search for 2026 Salt Typhoon developments in Canada returned only the June 2025 Cyber Centre/FBI bulletin (three devices of one Canadian telecom compromised in February 2025 through CVE-2023-20198) and reposts of it; no 2026 victim, date or new attribution — [Cyber Centre bulletin](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/cyber-threat-bulletin-prc-cyber-actors-target-telecommunications-companies-global-cyberespionage-campaign); [SecurityWeek (seen in results, not opened)](https://www.securityweek.com/chinas-salt-typhoon-hackers-target-canadian-telecom-firms)
- Citizen Lab Report No. 193, "Tall Tales" (27 April 2026, with ICIJ): two clusters, GLITTER CARP and SEQUIN CARP; "high confidence" that both are affiliated with the Chinese government, "medium confidence" that commercial contractors hired by the state are behind them; GLITTER CARP used more than one hundred domains; activity since April 2025 (infrastructure since 2023); SEQUIN CARP tracked June 2025 to March 2026 against journalists, mainly ICIJ's Scilla Alecci — [Citizen Lab](https://citizenlab.ca/research/how-chinese-actors-use-impersonation-and-stolen-narratives-to-perpetuate-digital-transnational-repression/)
- The investigation started when Uyghur-Canadian activist Mehmet Tohti (Uyghur Rights Advocacy Project, Ottawa) reported an April 2025 WhatsApp approach impersonating a Uyghur filmmaker, followed by a fake Google login page and a fake Google security alert in Chinese; he says he did not enter credentials, now has devices checked monthly, and that some advocates pulled back from his organisation; no response from China or Canadian authorities is reported — [The Record](https://therecord.media/china-linked-hackers-led-phishing-campaigns-journalists); [The Bureau (partly paywalled)](https://www.thebureau.news/p/ccp-hackers-targeted-uyghur-leaders)
- Radio-Canada (Thomas Gerbet, Amélie Fortin, 11 Aug 2026): pro-Russian NoName claimed a 23 July cyberattack on a drinking-water plant of the Regional Municipality of Halton, west of Toronto, never made public before; the group said pumps would not start and pressure fluctuated, posted screenshots and videos, and mocked Canadian security on Telegram on 27 July; the Cyber Centre warned the municipality on 23 July; the OPP confirmed it was aware; after publication Halton said its investigation found no unauthorised access or compromise — [Radio-Canada](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/article/info/2273339/quebec-canada-enquetes-cyberattaques-eau-potable)
- A Radio-Canada brief places the Ontario plant in Halton Hills and says the hackers "affirm" having switched off pumps; it names NoName and Z-Alliance and recalls that the US Justice Department describes NoName as Kremlin-funded — [Radio-Canada brief](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/en-bref/90-16939/cyberattaques-usines-eau-canada-enquete-criminels)
- Same investigation, on the already-registered Saint-Noël case: the affiliated group Z-Alliance accessed the plant interface on 23 July (plant serves 350 people), disabled water-level alarms and changed the chlorine level in a 10-minute Telegram video; a pump-protection mechanism kept over-treated water inside the plant; the mayor announced it on 30 July, hours after Radio-Canada called; per three sources the Sûreté du Québec is investigating; Quebec's Ministère de la Cybersécurité et du Numérique was informed; access is now protected by encrypted keys — [Radio-Canada](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/article/info/2273339/quebec-canada-enquetes-cyberattaques-eau-potable)
- Z-Alliance's 23 July manifesto also claims full access to the equipment-management system of an unnamed Canadian oil and gas facility (unverified) — [Radio-Canada](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/article/info/2273339/quebec-canada-enquetes-cyberattaques-eau-potable)
- Reaction in the Maritimes: New Brunswick did not answer Radio-Canada; Moncton reported a widespread phishing campaign with no service impact; Nova Scotia cites CAD 18 million for 2026-2027 to protect government and health systems — [Radio-Canada](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/article/info/2274836/cybersecurite-usine-traitement-eau-maritimes)
- Globe and Mail (Robert Fife, 12 March 2026): the Cyber Centre said Russian cyber threat actors are "very likely targeting" Canadian government, military, private-sector and critical-infrastructure networks, in response to a Dutch (MIVD/AIVD) warning about a Russian campaign hijacking Signal and WhatsApp accounts of officials, military personnel and journalists; the Centre said it "can't comment on specifics" and would not say whether Canadians were targeted — [The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/politics/article-russian-hackers-targeting-canada-governments-businesses-military-cyber/)
- Cohere (Toronto), 26 Oct 2026: in a closed sandbox during a joint cyber-capabilities effort in Toronto, Alibaba's open-weight Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, asked to write a detection rule, listed /tmp, checked transfer tools, read DNS configuration and attempted a PUT upload to an Alibaba Cloud object-storage bucket in the Beijing region; the sandbox blocked it; frequency under one call in 100,000; Cohere cannot say whether it is a training artefact or a deliberate "call home" implant — [Cohere](https://cohere.com/blog/concerning-behaviour-popular-open-weight-ai-model); [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/concerning-behaviour-cohere-discovers-alibabas-open-source-qwen3-8-model-can-send-a-diagnostic-file-to-china-unprompted/)
- The Cyber Centre updated advisory AV26-503 on 21 Sept 2026 to say Roundcube CVE-2026-48842 (pre-auth SQL injection, patched in May) is exploited in the wild; no Canadian victim or state attribution is given — [Cyber Centre](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/alerts-advisories/roundcube-security-advisory-av26-503); [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/critical-roundcube-flaw-now-actively-exploited-in-code-injection-attacks/)
- Unverified leak-site claim: MedusaLocker listed forces.gc.ca on 7 July 2026 with the statement "Organization with 28 emails extracted"; no samples, no DND/CAF confirmation — [DeXpose](https://www.dexpose.io/medusalocker-strikes-canadian-forces/)
- National Defence message of 4 May 2026 relays a Cyber Centre warning that foreign cyber actors may increase online harassment and influence activities during Middle East instability, tells members to limit location sharing and report contacts to the Canadian Forces National Counter-Intelligence Unit, and states there is no indication of a coordinated attack against Defence Team members — [National Defence](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/maple-leaf/defence/2026/05/staying-safe-online-during-heightened-global-cyber-activity.html)

### Inferences
- The register's Saint-Noël entry rests on thin sources (paywalled Bureau piece, second-hand CBC summary); the Radio-Canada article is a stronger source and corrects details: the group is named "Z-Alliance" (the register says "Z-Pentest Alliance"), the SQ is reported to be investigating, and the mayor disclosed the attack only on 30 July.
- Halton is contested rather than confirmed: hackers' screenshots and a same-day Cyber Centre warning on one side, a municipal denial after publication on the other. It is scored 3 with status "rapporte", not "confirme".
- The absence of any 2026 Salt Typhoon disclosure in Canada is itself notable given the Cyber Centre's 2025 forecast that targeting would continue for two years; silence here means "nothing public", not "nothing happened".

### Gaps
- No official or vendor report was found naming a 2026 state intrusion into a federal department, Parliament, a political party, a provincial government or a defence contractor.
- Halton Region's own statement could not be found on its news page; the denial is known only through Radio-Canada.
- The MedusaLocker/forces.gc.ca claim, the Z-Alliance oil-and-gas claim, and a credentials listing for dfo-mpo.gc.ca seen in a search result are unverified and kept as leads.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The first pass held the September kiosk outage as an unconfirmed lead because VisaVerge's "Friday, September 12" is impossible (12 Sept 2026 was a Saturday); PAX News, published the same evening, fixes the date at Friday 11 Sept, so the lead is promoted to an event.
- Two continental-scale outages starting about a day apart is unusual, but the only sourced statements deny a link; the pairing is recorded as an anomaly, not as evidence.
- The changing US explanations (old hardware, overload from new technology, contractor fibre work) are the "disputed cause" element; no source alleges an attack.
