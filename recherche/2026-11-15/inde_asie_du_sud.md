## 1. Hardeep Singh Nijjar case: prosecution in 2026 and new information on alleged Indian government links

### Takeaway
The Canadian prosecution of the four alleged hitmen produced almost no public movement in 2026 beyond a federal bid to keep some material secret. The big developments came from outside the courtroom: a Globe and Mail leak (1–2 March) alleging Indian consular/RAW involvement, and a U.S. indictment (7 July) charging Lawrence Bishnoi and Goldy Brar with ordering the killing without alleging any state role. The RCMP then said nothing in that probe linked the Indian government, which Sikh groups called a "complete U-turn" and New Delhi publicly welcomed.

### Cited Findings
- 19 Feb 2026: the federal Attorney General applied to the Federal Court under the Canada Evidence Act to confirm a prohibition on disclosing some information in the trial of the four accused, citing protection of international relations and national security; the AG had received four notices in 2024–2025 flagging sensitive or potentially injurious information. The four Indian nationals (charged May 2024 with murder and conspiracy) are before B.C. Supreme Court in New Westminster; the CP story gives no trial date. — [The Canadian Press via CKPG](https://ckpgtoday.ca/?p=482314)
- 1–2 Mar 2026: the Globe and Mail, citing two unnamed national-security/law-enforcement sources, reported that Canadian officials received evidence that Indian consular staff in Vancouver supplied information that helped the 2023 killing. A visa officer said to be a RAW officer allegedly gathered information on Nijjar from the Surrey diaspora, working with the then consul general; the information allegedly went to Vikash Yadav in New Delhi, who relayed it to the Bishnoi gang; a Canada-based gang member allegedly helped arrange the murder. The sources said this rests on RCMP investigations plus Canadian, U.S. and U.K. intelligence. — [Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/latest/1091101/scroll_in); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/3/2/sikh-group-in-canada-slams-india-over-new-report-into-2023-activist-killing)

### Inferences
- The public record now contains three incompatible framings of the same murder: state-directed via consular intelligence (Globe sources, March), gang-ordered with no state role alleged (U.S. indictment, July), and a gang acting for the Indian government (RCMP assessment released in January; see section 2). None has been tested in a Canadian court.
- Moreland's statement was scoped to the Hard Ball investigation, yet it was reported and then used by New Delhi as a general exoneration. That gap between what was said and how it travelled is itself a notable anomaly.
- The Federal Court secrecy application, on international-relations grounds, lands in the same weeks as the diplomatic reset; whatever its routine justification, it means evidence about a possible state sponsor may never be aired at trial.

### Gaps
- No 2026 trial date, pre-trial ruling, publication-ban ruling or Federal Court decision could be found; one aggregator snippet said the trial was expected to begin later in 2026, but this was not verifiable.
- The Globe and Mail original could not be opened; secondary reports say it named the visa officer, which I did not verify and do not repeat.
- The three people arrested in Canada under Hard Ball are not identified in the CP dispatches I opened; whether extradition is sought is unknown.

## 2. Bishnoi gang and the extortion/shooting wave: what police and documents said about Indian government links; arrests, deportations, terrorist-listing consequences

### Takeaway
The unverified lead is confirmed: on 13 Jan 2026 Global News published an RCMP national-security assessment, released under Access to Information, saying the Bishnoi gang uses violence while "acting on behalf of" the Indian government. Operational policing in 2026, however, treated extortion as multi-group organized crime, rarely mentioned the gang or India, and leaned on immigration removals; I found no terrorism charge flowing from the September 2025 listing.

### Cited Findings

- There is a visible split between the RCMP's national-security assessment (gang acts for India) and its front-line messaging (multiple groups, copycats, no state named). Either the assessment was thinly sourced, as Eby claimed, or operational spokespeople are deliberately staying silent on attribution.
- Removals are the main disruption tool (55 removals nationally by May). Fast removal of low-level shooters may reduce violence but also removes people who could testify about who directs them.
- The controlled entry of a suspect from India implies some level of Indian-side cooperation or at least non-interference, consistent with the liaison-officer arrangement agreed in February.

### Gaps

- I found no 2026 extortion data for Edmonton, Calgary or Brampton comparable to Surrey's, and no CSIS statement specifically on the gang.
- Whether the January RCMP assessment still reflects RCMP thinking after July's remarks is not publicly stated.

## 4. The Canada–India diplomatic reset: inconsistencies between political messaging and security-agency assessments

### Takeaway
The unverified lead is confirmed and is the central inconsistency of the year: on 25 Feb 2026 an unnamed senior official told reporters Ottawa was confident India's violent activity was not continuing, and within days CSIS said its assessment was unchanged, Liberal MPs and a secretary of state contradicted the claim, and the Prime Minister said he would not use those words. The pattern then repeated in milder form with the RCMP Commissioner (19 March) and the RCMP Deputy Commissioner (7 July), while the CSIS Public Report 2025 kept India on the list of main perpetrators but recast its conduct as historical.

### Cited Findings
- 6–7 Feb 2026: India's NSA Ajit Doval met Public Safety Minister Anandasangaree and NSIA Nathalie Drouin in Ottawa; they agreed a shared workplan on national security and law enforcement, liaison officers, information-sharing on fentanyl precursors and transnational organized crime, and cyber cooperation. - 25 Feb 2026 briefing: the official said the government was confident the activity was not continuing and that Carney would not travel if Canada believed India was actively interfering. — [The Pointer](https://thepointer.com/article/2026-02-27/as-carney-walks-the-fence-in-brampton-across-the-country-sikh-canadians-others-don-t-buy-claim-that-india-has-stopped-its-campaign-of-violence-here); [CP via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/02/25/sikh-canadians-say-state-violence-a-continued-threat-ahead-of-carney-going-to-india/)
